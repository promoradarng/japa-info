US says it has cancelled over 6,000 visas linked to birth tourism

USA · Visa warning · 13 Nov 2026

USAVisa warning

The US State Department's Birth Tourism Prevention Task Force says it has now revoked more than 6,000 visas held by people who travelled to give birth in America so the child gets US citizenship, or who helped arrange such trips. A month earlier the figure was about 4,000, and the department says it is still identifying others. It did not say how many of those affected were Nigerian.

Japa wise

Never use a US visitor visa to travel for childbirth or to help someone else do so; it can cost you the visa and future applications.

Court don ruleUS court strikes down four-year cap on student visa stays

A federal judge in Massachusetts has thrown out the Homeland Security rule that would have given F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors and foreign journalists a fixed admission period instead of 'duration of status'. The court had already paused the rule in September, and on 16 November it cancelled it completely. For now, students can stay as long as they keep studying full time on a valid I-20, but the government can still appeal.

New formUS now requires a health declaration from every air passenger

From 3 November, everyone flying to the United States, whatever their nationality, must sign a CDC form saying they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous 21 days. Airlines must check the form before boarding, and it also applies to people connecting through a US airport. The order is set to run until 18 December unless CDC changes it.

Fixed stayUS ends open-ended student stay: F-1 degree students get 4 years

A US Homeland Security rule that took effect on 15 September 2026 replaces the old 'duration of status' with a fixed end date on the I-94 record. Degree students get up to four years and language students up to 24 months. Staying longer means applying to USCIS for an extension, and a refusal can leave a student with no grace period. Update: a federal court paused this rule on 14 September and struck it down on 16 November 2026, so duration of status still applies for now.
