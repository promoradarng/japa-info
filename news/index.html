CanadaRule change

Job offer don returnCanada's Express Entry gives job offers points again, no LMIA needed

Since 3 November, Express Entry has a new 'labour market integration' score worth up to 150 points. A job offer in a high-wage occupation now earns 50, 70 or 100 points depending on how well the job pays, and it no longer needs an LMIA; Canadian work experience in such jobs can score instead, but you get only the higher of the two. On 25 November IRCC invited 6,000 French speakers with a cut-off score of 401, the second draw under the new system.

NigeriaRule change

Made in NaijaNigeria to print its own passport booklets from May 2027

Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo broke ground on 20 November on a $70 million passport booklet factory at the Nigeria Immigration Service headquarters in Abuja, and said local production should start by May 2027. The plant is meant to make six million e-passport booklets a year at first, well above Nigeria's yearly need of about two million, so booklet shortages caused by foreign suppliers and exchange rates should ease. Travel wahalaAustralia tightens travel permission for sponsored workers waiting on visas

Home Affairs says Bridging Visa B applications lodged from 30 November 2026 by people waiting on an employer-sponsored visa will get travel periods that match the actual trip, not the long periods of up to a year or more that were common. USAStudents

Internship reprieveUS judge blocks new limits on student internships (CPT)

A federal judge in Boston has stopped the Department of Homeland Security from enforcing two August 2026 directives that narrowed curricular practical training (CPT), the work placements F-1 students do as part of their course. Under the directives, a placement only counted if every student in the programme had to do it, which shut out elective internships taken for credit. The order is a preliminary injunction, so the case continues and the government could still appeal.

New ZealandStudents

Now openNew Zealand's six-month graduate work visa is now open

Since 16 November 2026, international graduates of Level 5 to 7 courses in New Zealand who studied full-time for at least 24 weeks, but whose qualification doesn't qualify for a Post-Study Work Visa, can apply online for a Short-Term Graduate Work Visa. It gives six months of open work rights to job-hunt and move to an employer-sponsored visa, and you need at least NZ$5,000 to support yourself. You must apply within three months of your student visa ending, you get only one, and English, foundation and bridging courses don't count.

AustraliaRule change

No switchingAustralia visitor visas lodged from 28 November will carry 'No Further Stay'

From 28 November 2026, condition 8503 ('No Further Stay') becomes compulsory on Australian Visitor (subclass 600) tourist and business visas, as well as ETA and eVisitor visas. Anyone holding such a visa will generally be unable to apply for another visa, such as a student or work visa, while inside Australia. It only covers applications lodged on or after 28 November, and a waiver is possible only after a major change in circumstances you could not have foreseen.

AustraliaRule change

Family door closeAustralia stops graduates adding family to 485 visas later

From 13 November 2026, partners and children can only be included in a Temporary Graduate (subclass 485) visa if they apply together with the graduate in one combined application. In most cases they must already have been on the graduate's Student or 485 visa as dependants, or be a child born after that visa was granted; joining later as a subsequent entrant is no longer possible. Applications lodged before 13 November are decided under the old rules.

AustraliaRule change

Apply from outsideAustralia's Training visa (407) now only for applicants outside Australia

From 12 November 2026, a new application for Australia's Training visa (subclass 407) is only valid if you lodge it while outside Australia, and you must also be outside Australia when the visa is granted. An application made from inside Australia under the new rule is treated as invalid and gets no bridging visa, even if the online system accepts it. Valid applications lodged before 12 November are still decided under the old rules, and existing 407 visas are not cancelled.

Pikin firstNew Zealand won't grant parents residence while a child is unlawful

Immigration New Zealand says that for residence applications lodged from 12 November 2026, a parent or other secondary applicant cannot be approved while a dependent child is in the country without a valid visa. The child must first get a proper visa or leave New Zealand, and dropping the child from the application does not get around the rule. Applications lodged before 12 November are decided under the old rules.
