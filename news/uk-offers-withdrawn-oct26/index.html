More Nigerian UK study applications withdrawn as universities fear refusals

UK · Students · 28 Oct 2026

UKStudents

A report citing a Financial Times analysis of Home Office data says 22.7% of Nigerian student visa applications were withdrawn in the first half of 2026, up from 4.5% a year earlier. The report links the jump to universities pulling offers early, because they must now keep visa refusals under 5% or risk losing their licence. The figures are secondhand, so treat them as a trend, not an official count.

Japa wise

Hold your CAS and visa application until you have clear funds proof and strong study ties, and confirm your offer is still live before paying deposits.

One week leftUK student visa funds go up on 30 November

From Sunday 30 November, UK student visa applicants must show more money for living costs: £1,570 a month for study in London (up from £1,529) and £1,203 a month elsewhere (up from £1,171), for up to nine months. The change comes from the Home Office's September statement of changes (HC 584). Applications made before 30 November are judged on the old amounts.

Prepare well oUK judged 84% of interviewed Nigerian students not credible

A UK Visas and Immigration briefing seen by The PIE News shows caseworkers found 9,043 of the 11,415 student visa applicants they interviewed between May and October 2026 not credible. For Nigerian applicants the figure was 84%, behind only Nepal and India, and the share of Nigerians called for interview jumped from 7% in 2025 to 21% this year. The most common weak points were little research into the university, not explaining why the UK and the course fit future plans, poor course knowledge, English level and doubts about funds.
