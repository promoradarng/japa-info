STEM draw don land

Canada holds first Express Entry STEM draw since 2024, cut-off 547

On 26 November IRCC invited 600 Express Entry candidates in STEM occupations to apply for permanent residence, the first STEM-only round since April 2024. The lowest score invited was 547, far above the 491 cut-off of the last STEM draw, and it came a day after a French-language round that invited 6,000 people at 401. Draws had paused in early November while IRCC rescored profiles under its new points system. Read more

UKStudents

Numbers don dropUK study visas down 23% as net migration falls to 77,000

New Home Office figures published on 26 November show the UK granted about 336,000 sponsored study visas in the year to September 2026, 23% fewer than a year earlier, while Skilled Worker visas fell 27% and Health and Care Worker visas fell 44%. On the same day the ONS estimated net migration at about 77,000 for the year to June 2026, the lowest in over a decade. The UK still granted 2.2 million visitor visas in the same period.

CanadaRule change

Job offer don returnCanada's Express Entry gives job offers points again, no LMIA needed

Since 3 November, Express Entry has a new 'labour market integration' score worth up to 150 points. A job offer in a high-wage occupation now earns 50, 70 or 100 points depending on how well the job pays, and it no longer needs an LMIA; Canadian work experience in such jobs can score instead, but you get only the higher of the two. On 25 November IRCC invited 6,000 French speakers with a cut-off score of 401, the second draw under the new system.

UKTiming

One week leftUK student visa funds go up on 30 November

From Sunday 30 November, UK student visa applicants must show more money for living costs: £1,570 a month for study in London (up from £1,529) and £1,203 a month elsewhere (up from £1,171), for up to nine months. The change comes from the Home Office's September statement of changes (HC 584). Applications made before 30 November are judged on the old amounts.

NigeriaRule change

Made in NaijaNigeria to print its own passport booklets from May 2027

Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo broke ground on 20 November on a $70 million passport booklet factory at the Nigeria Immigration Service headquarters in Abuja, and said local production should start by May 2027. The plant is meant to make six million e-passport booklets a year at first, well above Nigeria's yearly need of about two million, so booklet shortages caused by foreign suppliers and exchange rates should ease. It is privately funded under a concession, and the new factory alone does not promise faster processing.

New ZealandTiming

System go slowNew Zealand visa portal down Sunday after Microsoft outage pushed upgrade back a week

Immigration New Zealand says many online visa applications, including student, most visitor, AEWV and partner or child of a worker or student visas, cannot be started or continued from 08:00 to 23:59 NZ time on Sunday 22 November. Some others, including partner or child of a New Zealander visas, go offline briefly between 06:00 and 08:00 on Monday 23 November. The upgrade was first planned for 15–16 November but slipped a week after a global Microsoft outage disrupted INZ systems.
